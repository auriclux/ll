How this site came to be

On the morning of November 7, 2026, I was texting my nephew John about an orbital robot assembling a perimeter truss in low Earth orbit — a machine out of a scenario of mine called Tidy Skies. John wrote back, "we do all of this but to what end," and then, "what a strange way to live." I answered that his line was a title to a book I would pick up, except there won't be books; there will be links, and a link carries no gravitas; and soon enough, with implants, not even a click — just let me think that topic.

I had been emailing friends and family the long summaries and narratives that come out of my discussions with AI, as attachments. Attachments are where writing goes to be admired and unread. I wanted addresses instead: a link I could put in a text, which would arrive wearing the piece's own face.

The venue question took a day. A dormant WordPress account could be resurrected, but its free pages carry someone else's ads. Bear Blog is a well-made thing run by one man, and its links would die on his schedule, not mine. So the pieces live here, in a repository of my own, as plain markdown files — the files are the site, everything else is a rendering — and if this host ever needs replacing, the work flows out to the next one because I own the canon.

The first leaf on the shelf is Tidy Skies itself, the scenario John and I were texting about. The snake eats its tail at the first meal; that seems correct.

This page is a living page. It grows as the site does.
