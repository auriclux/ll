## Flash forward: the game

November 2026. A man is playing Grand Theft Auto VI, and his hands are
empty.[^gta]

He is not holding a controller. There is nothing to hold. He imagines
tapping his ring finger, and on the screen his character throws a
punch. He imagines sliding his right hand forward, and the plane he is
flying pushes its nose up into the climb. He drives. He steers a
speedboat across open water. He gets into fights. Every input the game
has ever asked of thumbs and fingers, he supplies by intending the
movement and not making it, and the machine, reading the intention
where it forms, makes it for him.

Sit with the strangeness of that a moment, because it is the whole
leaf in one scene. A generation ago the hand held the object. Then the
finger tapped the link. Now the intention alone does the work, and the
body, the loyal machinery of every previous era, sits the scene out
like a spectator at its own life.

He is a clinical-trial participant, one man, playing one game, and
none of that diminishes it. The Wright brothers flew for twelve
seconds. What matters is not the duration. What matters is that the
door is open and a man has walked through it on camera.

## How I thought of it

This leaf began as a text exchange with my nephew John, who read a
paragraph I sent him about an orbital robot and answered with the
sentence that became the title. The exchange wandered, the way the
good ones do, from books to links to the question of what finding
things out even means when the finding gets effortless, and it left me
with a headache. Not a conclusion. A sensation: too many possibilities
zinging around at once, none of them willing to sit still and be
examined.

A first draft of this leaf sat in a drawer for two weeks, finished in
the way a thing is finished when it has stopped moving. It described
the eras of discovery and stopped, as if discovery were still the
subject. Then the video above crossed my desk, a day after the game
launched, and the drawer opened. The draft had stopped one era early.
The possibilities that gave me the headache were not a fog. They were
a crowd, and crowds can be organized. This second sitting is the
unwinding of that headache, one possibility at a time.

Here is the premise, plainly. In the implant society, *thinking*
delivers results that used to require physical agency. Think, and your
exoskeleton scoops you up and takes you for a walk. Think, and your
opto-cortical stimulators take you shopping in the bazaar at
Marrakech. Think, and you inhabit the humanoid in the octagon, trading
blows with a human fighter, feeling the canvas through feet that are
not yours. It takes "all in your head" to new levels, because the head
is no longer where things stop. It is where they start.


## The approach march

It started, as these things now do, with a text. I sent my nephew John
a paragraph about a robot launched to low Earth orbit in March,
assembling a perimeter truss in space, and asked if he recalled the
[Tidy Skies](https://auriclux.github.io/ll/tidy-skies) scenario: "(In a
movie opening Basso Profundo) 'It begins.'"

John: "we do all of this but to what end."

Me: "subservience to our synthetic overlords."

John: "Hmmmm what a strange way to live lol."

And then, at 7:52 in the morning, the thought that turned a joke into
this paper:

> "THAT's a title to a book I'd pick up. Wait, there won't be books. 'A
> link I'd click.' doesn't have the gravitas for me. AND when we all
> have those implants, it won't even be a click, just 'Let me think
> that topic.' I just gave myself another headache. And topic."

The headache deserves a correction before we go any further, because I
have since worked out what it was. It was not the thought of implants.
It was the sensation of too many possibilities arriving at once, zinging
around with nowhere to sit down. Hold that fact. This whole leaf is the
unwinding of that headache, one possibility at a time, until each has a
door to be behind.

I had described, without knowing it, an approach march. Three eras of
reaching the world, each one stripping a physical layer off the
reaching. My hand, then my finger, then nothing.

The book is the hand's era. A book is an object with gravitas: weight, a
spine, a jacket. A book is a shelf's unit of account. You can point at
it, lend it, lose it, find it again twenty years later in the bottom of
a musty trunk with the same pages. Finding things out in the book era
was a physical act with friction built in: you went to the shelf, or
the library, or the shop. The friction was the filter. Nobody accused a
book of being too available. And a book carries its citation in its
body: author, title, page. You can argue with a book by opening it to
the place where it says the thing. The argument has an address.

The book also carries a romance no successor has matched. Half the
fantasy and adventure stories ever filmed turn on the same hinge:
somewhere there is an arcane book, in a locked library or a drowned
vault, and the secret in it can change the world. That is the mystique
of the Library of Alexandria, carried forward two thousand years, the
idea that knowledge has a *place*, that it can be hidden, guarded,
stolen, burned. Discovery in the book era had geography. You could go
on a quest for a fact.

The link is the finger's era, and the link is weightless. It arrives by
text, it is clicked, and it never quite arrives at all, because there is
nothing to hold. "A link I'd click" does not have the gravitas, and the
difference is not snobbery. A link outsources the shelf. The shelf used
to be mine: my books, my order, my memory of where the good one lives.
The link lives on someone else's server, in someone else's order. Look
at my phone that morning as an exhibit: in the recents list, wedged
between mundane notes, sits a deploy text from a piece of software I
was building. Nobody sent me that link. A build system did. It is no
longer a human handing me something to follow; it is a process notifying
me that a thing exists. Nobody curated that shelf. It accumulated. And
the citation broke quietly on the way: a link rots, moves, paywalls,
returns a different page than the one your friend read. You can still
argue with it, but the address keeps changing. A link has no geography,
no vault, no quest. Nobody will ever make a movie about finding a
hyperlink in a musty trunk.

The thought is the third era, and it removes the finger. "Let me think
that topic." No shelf, no click, no gesture at all. The topic arrives
because it was thought. What the thought era does to the things the book
gave us for free can be said in one pass. Serendipity: the shelf
surprised you by adjacency, the link surprises you by profiling, and the
thought cannot surprise you at all unless something outside your own
head injects the topic. Citation: the book cites by page, the link by
address, badly, and the thought by nothing; a thought you cannot source
is a rumor you had to yourself. Ownership: I own my books, I rent my
links, and my retrieved thoughts would be the most intimate thing I own
and the most plausibly not mine. Attention: the book demanded an
evening, the link demands a click, the thought demands nothing, which
is the problem. A discovery flow with zero cost is a flow with zero
filter.

Three eras, then, and each one cheaper than the last. Hand, finger,
nothing. It reads like a story about finding things out, and for
sixty-odd years of my life it was one. But the march has been doing
something while nobody watched the direction. Every step stripped a
layer of the body out of *discovery*, and discovery was never the only
thing the body was for. The same channel that learned to fetch a topic
can fetch anything else a body used to fetch. Think your exoskeleton to
scoop you up and take you for a walk. Think your opto-cortical
stimulators to take you shopping at the bazaar of Marrakech. Inhabit
the humanoid in the octagon, trading blows with a human fighter, and
feel the crowd through its ears. It takes "all in your head" to new
levels. The man in the video is not discovering anything. He is
*acting*, at a distance, with his body sitting still. The march was
never only about the library.

So the march continues past the library, and it comes to a wall.

## The hallway

The march ends at a wall, and the wall opens. Behind it: the hallway.
The wider effects of the implant, one possibility per door. A mnemonic.
It works.

The doors carry signs. Brass plates, titles cut into them, and the
near ones can be read as you pass: Cerebro-Hacking. Functional
Telepathy. Assemblies. Intended Words. Porn. The lit doors are the
ones already cracked open in laboratories, with dates. Further down,
the plates take more squinting: The Filter. The Siren. Brain
Influencers. Thoughtcaster. Inhabiting Alternates. One, in the dim stretch, reads Hypnos.
Past that the titles continue into darkness, uncounted, and I will not
sign for a number.

This leaf only walks the hallway. It opens nothing. Each door gets its
own leaf in time, opened and explored on its own terms, and this
prologue will have done its work if the reader can find the doors
again. Walk it with me. The doors stand in the order their first
openings happened, because that is the only order I trust.

**Cerebro-Hacking, 2012.** A cheap headset and a malicious app farmed
the brain's recognition signal for private facts. The membrane between
minds gets attacked first.[^p300][^frank]

**Functional Telepathy, 2013.** One man imagined moving his hand.
Another man's finger, across campus, moved. The receiver called it a
tic.[^rao][^brainnet]

**Assemblies, 2019.** Telepathy that stays switched on: linked people,
membership and hierarchy carried by the link. Some beneficial. Some
cults.

**Intended Words, 2023.** Attempted speech decoded at seventy-eight
words a minute; a voice restored. Only what you try to say leaves. For
now.[^speech]

**Porn.** The oldest early adopter meets the first medium that is
inhabited, not watched. The distance between desire and experience
closes to zero.

Then the plates on doors that have not opened:

**The Filter.** The membrane deciding what enters a mind and what
leaves it. Its failure sounds like cacophony: every linked mind, at
once.

**The Siren.** A cerebro-hacker working through desire, a signal the
filter passes because the receiver wants it. The will is outbid, not
overridden.

**Inhabiting Alternates.** The interface that imagines hands can
imagine tentacles: alternate physiologies, worn in alternate
environments. The body becomes a choice of equipment.

**Brain Influencers.** Fame remeasured in links: not followers who can
look away, but minds yours is present inside.

**Thoughtcaster.** The professional form: a performer of inner life,
thought at by an audience that invited him in.

And in the dim stretch, one plate I can read and will not open:

**Hypnos.** The watchman dismissed, communication entering uninspected.
No precedent, no mechanism. Named, and not opened.

Past Hypnos the plates continue into darkness, uncounted. The headache
was what it felt like to hear them all at once, before any of them had
a door to be behind.

## The line

One honest paragraph before the hallway swallows the light, because a
door back in the lit stretch is still standing open on it.

In 2013 a man's finger moved on another man's thought, and the man
whose finger it was did not decide to move it. Call that what it is: a
small act of control, one bit wide, delivered through a communication
channel. This branch is built on the line that the implant society is
about communication and not control, and the 2013 experiment means the
line cannot rest on physics. The channel *can* move a body. It has.

The line rests instead on scope and consent, and it is a better line
for standing on ground that can be defended. One bit, invited,
announced, inside a game two volunteers agreed to play, is
communication with an unusual accent. A channel wide enough to carry
a command, opened without invitation, is the thing the line exists to
refuse. Between those two points lies most of the politics of the
implant society, and several doors in the hallway are just that
distance, measured from different ends.

The fiction writers, who furnished the rooms early, mostly drew the
line in the frightening place: the hive that absorbs you, the filter
that edits you. The laboratories, arriving later, have so far drawn it
in the hopeful one: attempted speech only, intended movement only,
what you try to say is what leaves. Both are reports from the same
hallway. This leaf's wager is that the line holds where the second
group is standing, and that the first group serves best as the
standing warning on the wall, which is where warnings belong.

John asked, at the start of all this, what we do all of this *to what
end*. I do not have the end. I have a hallway, a headache unwound into
doors, and a nephew who will argue about every plate on every one of
them. What a strange way to live. It is also, on the evidence, the way
we were always going to.
